# District Almanac Data Release — November 3, 2026

This is a shared, source-backed addition to every U.S. House district page; no district-specific conclusions are fabricated.

## Source-backed coverage
- 435 district route IDs, with actual U.S. Census community or local-neighborhood reference points.
- 435 historical ACS 2020–2024 119th-Congress district records: population, median household income, and bachelor's-or-higher share, with national and within-state ranking calculated from the same historical vintage.
- 2012, 2014, 2016 House elections from the MEDSL historical archive and 2018, 2020, 2022, 2024 from the existing reviewed historical-contests ledger.
- 413 district identifiers have all seven historical elections in these archives; 22 have gaps, often because those seats did not exist before reapportionment or older returns were unavailable.
- 2024 certified finalist names/votes retained for seven separately sourced contested general elections with no D/R pair.
- Current official 2026 candidate snapshots shown only where a state qualified-ballot list was previously reviewed (151 district offices in the existing atlas).

## Editorial limits
- Identical district numbers across 2010/2020 reapportionment do not certify identical geography. All pre-2022 returns are labeled the old map era.
- Districts in states with redraws in 2024 or 2026 require district-specific checks. We publish historical results as historical results, not updated vote estimates.
- A Census reference-point-in-district does not mean an entire city or neighborhood lies within that district; list order is not a ranking of population.
- Median household income is an ACS estimate with published margin of error; bachelor's rate MOE has not been recalculated.
- Old FEC filings alone do not constitute ballot qualification. Unverified 2026 candidates are not promoted to the ballot roster.
- These archived datasets and source snapshots are not live Election Day results.

## Next original data collection milestones
1. Expand ACS beyond the three existing measures with state and Census Bureau original data: poverty, age, housing costs, employment, educational attainment by category, broadband, veterans, and commuting; save source variables, vintage, error margins, and changed-geography cautions for each.
2. Build accurate district-to-county **area and population** overlap via official Census block geography, not only district-reference points.
3. Add each district's historical/economic identity, significant industries, employers and relevant infrastructure using source-verified local records, one geographic batch at a time.
4. Expand publisher-linked local reporting beyond the IA-01 feed. Never relabel state-wide or national stories as district reporting.
5. Add current officeholder/legislative votes from original congressional roll calls, while respecting as-of election dates and not treating candidates as incumbent lawmakers.

The entire almanac is rendered through politics-district-almanac.js and its own native CSS file, not through a new layer of global CSS overrides.
